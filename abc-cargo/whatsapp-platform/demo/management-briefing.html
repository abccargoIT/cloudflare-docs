Bringing WhatsApp customer service in-house

A proposal to move ABC Cargo's three regional WhatsApp numbers from Freshworks onto a platform we own, build and control — without interrupting a single customer conversation.

Prepared by ABC Cargo IT Department For Management review Date 5 September 2026 Status Proposal, no live change made
Nothing live has been touched
01

What is being asked of Management

Approve the preparation work, not the go-live.

This briefing asks for approval to build and prove the platform. It does not ask for permission to switch any customer-facing number today. A separate approval is requested later, only after Management has seen the finished platform working in a demonstration.

Decision 1

Proceed with the build. Authorise the IT Department to construct the platform and prove it against test numbers.

Decision 2

Confirm the demonstration gate. No customer number moves until Management has seen the platform demonstrated and signed off.

Decision 3

Decide the future of Freshworks. Retired entirely, or retained for email, phone and ticketing. This changes the licence saving materially.

02

Why change anything

WhatsApp is where a large share of ABC Cargo's customers already ask where their shipment is. Today those conversations run through Freshworks, and three things limit what we can do with them.

Automation is barely used

2 of 9 groups

Only UK Customer Support and UAE Sales Team assign conversations automatically. The other seven rely on an agent noticing the message, which is the most likely cause of slow first replies.

No link to shipment data

Manual

An agent answering "where is my shipment" looks the reference up by hand in another system. The most common question we receive is also the least automated.

Our customer data sits in a vendor

362K+

Resolved conversations held in a platform we rent. We cannot query it freely, and we cannot join it to our own cargo systems.

Owning the platform lets us answer the tracking question automatically, notify customers the moment a shipment status changes, and keep the conversation history alongside our own operational data.

03

What we would build

A single platform for all three regions, with the same shape of tooling our agents use today, plus the integration Freshworks cannot give us.

For agents

  • Shared inbox per region
  • Customer, shipment and consent visible beside the conversation
  • Automatic assignment and clear ownership
  • Templates for anything outside the 24-hour reply window

For operations

  • Shipment status changes trigger a WhatsApp update automatically
  • Tracking questions answered without an agent
  • Delivery and document requests sent on real events
  • Each region keeps its own hours, groups and targets

For management

  • Volume, response times and automation coverage per region
  • One consolidated view across UAE, UK and KSA
  • Full conversation history retained by ABC Cargo
  • No per-seat licence as the team grows
04

See it working

Both of these run entirely in a browser against sample data. Neither contacts Meta, Freshworks or any ABC Cargo system.

The platform → Inbox, contacts, broadcasts, automation rules, reports and the admin surface, with UAE, UK and KSA separated throughout. How a message is handled → A customer sends a message and every step the platform runs is shown as it happens, including the out-of-hours reply and the 24-hour window closing.
Suggested walkthrough for the meeting
  • Open the platform on Inbox and show a tracking enquiry with the shipment already attached.
  • Switch the number between UAE, UK and KSA to show each region is genuinely separate.
  • Open Admin and show the regional configuration table: different hours, groups and SLA per region.
  • Open Reports for volume and response times by region.
  • In the second demo, press +24 hours and show the reply box lock itself, which is the Meta rule that governs the whole design.
05

How it would be delivered

Nothing customer-facing changes until the platform is finished and Management has seen it. After that, regions move one at a time, smallest first, so the fewest customers are exposed to the first switch.

Indicative schedule · approximately seven months

Preparation, nothing customer-facing KSA UAE UK Dashed line: nothing goes live before this point
Stage Duration What happens Customer impact
Baseline and exports 2 weeks Export the current Freshworks configuration and history. Confirm shipment reference format and system access. None
Build the platform 10 weeks Construct and test everything against test numbers only. Agents keep working in Freshworks throughout. None
Management demonstration Gate Full working platform shown to Management. Go-live approval requested here, and not before. Decision point
KSA go-live 3 weeks Smallest region first. Three agents, nine open conversations. Proves the platform on real traffic. Smallest region
UAE go-live 4 weeks Thirteen agents. Tracking automation and shipment integration prove their value here. Planned switch

Durations are the IT Department's estimate for planning purposes. They are not a commitment until the baseline exports in stage one are complete.

What it costs, honestly

The IT Department cannot see the licence figures, so none are invented here.

The table below sets out what has to be compared. Finance holds the Freshworks figures. Where a number is not known to IT, the row says so rather than guessing.

Item Today After the change Source of the figure
Agent licences, 40 users Freshworks subscription None Finance to supply the current annual cost
WhatsApp message charges Paid to Meta Paid to Meta, unchanged Charged per template message regardless of platform
Hosting and infrastructure Included in subscription Cloudflare usage Modest at this volume; to be confirmed against current pricing
Build effort — One-off IT estimate: roughly seven months elapsed, one developer plus part-time IT
Ongoing maintenance Absorbed by the vendor Falls to ABC Cargo IT A real and continuing cost, not a saving
Freshworks for email and ticketing In use Decision 3 If retained, the licence saving is smaller than it first appears

The case for this project is capability and ownership rather than pure cost saving. Automatic shipment notifications and instant tracking answers are worth more than the licence line, and they are not available to us on the current setup. Management should weigh it on that basis.

Risks and how they are controlled

Risk Severity How it is controlled
A number is switched and customers cannot reach us High One region at a time, smallest first, verified on internal phones before the regional team is released to it
Conversation history left behind in Freshworks High Full export completed and verified before any number is disconnected. This step is irreversible if skipped
Agents lose familiar tooling Medium Training per region before its switch; the platform mirrors the working pattern agents already know

08

If approved, what happens next

  • Freshworks configuration and conversation history are exported and verified. No live change.
  • The platform is built and tested against test numbers only. No live change.
  • Management is shown the finished platform and asked to approve go-live. Decision point.
  • Only then does KSA move, followed by UAE and UK, one region at a time.
The commitment this briefing makes

No ABC Cargo customer number, Meta account or production system is touched until every preparation step is complete and Management has approved go-live in the demonstration. If approval is withheld, nothing has changed and Freshworks continues exactly as it does today.
